Which inverter do I need if I want a battery later?

Short answer

A hybrid inverter. It runs the panels, the battery and the grid from one unit, even if you are only fitting panels today. The battery can be added later without having to replace the inverter.

If you choose an ordinary inverter now, adding a battery later means buying a second unit you would not have needed. That is the most expensive decision you can make at the start of a project.

In plain language

Why the inverter decides everything.

What an inverter actually does

Your panels make a kind of electricity that no appliance in your house can use. The inverter converts it into the electricity that comes out of your socket. Everything your roof generates therefore passes through it.

Think of it as the engine of a car: the panels are the fuel, but the engine decides what you actually do with it. That is why this is the part where a cheap choice makes itself felt the longest.

“Hybrid” in plain language

A hybrid inverter can do one extra thing: it can also run a battery. You do not have to buy that battery today. You are only keeping the door open.

If you fit an ordinary inverter now and decide you want storage in three years' time, a second unit gets added to your board. That is money you need not have spent. This is why we always ask during the site inspection whether a battery is ever in your plans — even when the answer is “maybe”.

Why we do not choose on price here

Cheaper inverters exist. The problem is not the first day, it is year eight: a brand that has left the market no longer honours a warranty and no longer supplies parts. You are then left with a unit on your wall that nobody can repair.

We work with a listed manufacturer with a European product line, TÜV Rheinland certificates and CEI 0-21 through Kiwa. Not because certificates sound impressive, but because we are the ones who come back if something is wrong.

Shade on your roof

A chimney, a roof window or the neighbour's tree: with partial shade one panel can drag the rest of its string down with it. With per-panel control that does not happen.

Whether you need that depends on your roof and on the time of day that shade falls. We assess it on site rather than selling it as standard. More on this on the solar panels page.

Where does the inverter go, and does it make noise?

An inverter is mounted on a wall in the garage, utility room or plant room, as close as possible to the meter cupboard. It needs ventilation and does not like full sun.

We decide the exact position at your home during the site inspection, together with the cable route and the position of any battery.
