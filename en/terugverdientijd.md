> How long does it take to pay off solar panels in 2026? An explanation of net metering, self-consumption and the calculation in four steps.

# Working out payback time in 2026.

Payback time is how long it takes you to win back, through a lower bill, what the installation cost you. In 2026 that sum is done differently than it was a few years ago, and that is why the old rules of thumb no longer hold.

### What has changed

The meter that runs backwards no longer exists. It used to be that your meter turned back whenever you produced more than you used, and everything was cancelled out at the end of the year. That system, called net metering, has been abolished for good.

If you put electricity onto the grid today, you receive an injection fee for it, and that fee is clearly lower than what you pay to buy the same electricity back later. Electricity you use yourself, at the moment your panels are producing, is therefore worth a great deal more.

### The calculation in four steps

Step 1. What the installation actually costs you, VAT included. For a home older than ten years the reduced rate of 6 per cent applies; for a newer home or new build it is 21 per cent.

Step 2. How much you generate. Work with the yield calculated for your own roof, not with an average from a brochure.

Step 3. How much of that you use yourself. This figure decides everything. A household that is home during the day, or that has a battery, a heat pump or an EV charger, comes out much higher.

Step 4. Work out what you save each year. What you use yourself, you save at your full price per kWh; what you export earns only the injection fee. That sum divided into the investment gives your payback time.

### What shortens the payback time

Running your heavy appliances during the day: washing machine, tumble dryer, dishwasher.

A home battery, so that evening use still comes from your own production.

An electric car that charges during the day rather than overnight.

Controls that start those appliances automatically when the sun is shining.
